# D. Company and science information streams for Bobby Hedgefund

Research date: 2026-11-15. Read-only desk research plus 4 tiny anonymous test calls (Europe PMC, bioRxiv, NIH RePORTER, Crossref) made 2026-11-15 22:02 UTC. Not legal advice anywhere in this document.

Labels: **[verified]** = read on an official page or primary source today; **[secondary]** = law-firm, vendor or third-party page; **[unverified]** = from my background knowledge or my own estimate, not checked today. Volume numbers are my own rough estimates for ~200 small/mid-cap biotechs and are marked as such.

---

## 0. Answer first (summary)

1. **SEC EDGAR is the backbone and the best value for the effort.** It is free, has no key, and is point-in-time by design: every filing has an acceptance datetime and is never overwritten (amendments are new filings). Filings show up on sec.gov 1-3 minutes after the EDGAR timestamp. The rules: 10 requests/second at most, and a User-Agent header with a name and contact e-mail. The owner has to decide what contact string to send.
2. **Fastest open way to catch material news:** company IR-site RSS feeds plus EDGAR "latest filings" Atom. Newswire RSS is technically fastest, but PR Newswire's terms ban robots outright, and GlobeNewswire's/Notified's terms ban bots for the Service. Business Wire's terms do mention "retrieving RSS feeds" as an allowed use. EDGAR 8-K copies of press releases often arrive the same day but can legally lag (four business days for most items; "promptly" under Reg FD).
3. **Science:** Europe PMC REST (no key, includes preprints) and the bioRxiv/medRxiv API are the easiest wins. PubMed E-utilities works without a key at 3 requests/second (10 with a free key). OpenAlex changed its model in 2026: usage is now priced and a free key gives 10x the budget, so treat it as a secondary source. Lens.org API now needs an institutional subscription, so avoid it.
4. **Conference abstracts move small-biotech prices** on fixed, published embargo-lift times (e.g. ASH 2026: regular abstracts went online 4 Nov 2026 9:00 ET; late-breakers 7 Dec 2026 9:00 ET). There is no open aggregated feed, and ASH forbids public republication without consent.
5. **Evidence for filing signals:** solid in general markets for "opportunistic" insider buys (Cohen, Malloy, Pomorski 2012) and for first-time going-concern opinions (−14% drift over a year; Kausar, Taffler, Tan 2009). Evidence is thin and biotech-specific only through vendor backtests for specialist-fund 13F ownership. For ATM programmes there is a descriptive academic study but I found no biotech return evidence.

- **Fair access [verified]:** "current maximum access rate is 10 requests per second". You must declare a User-Agent, sample "Sample Company Name AdminContact@<sample company domain>.com". Automated crawling of the site is not allowed. The SEC can lower the limits. Sources: [Accessing EDGAR data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data), [Webmaster FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/about/webmaster-frequently-asked-questions), [EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces). No CORS on data.sec.gov. **Owner decision:** what contact string goes in the User-Agent (it must contain an e-mail; a dedicated project address keeps the personal one out).
- **Dissemination [verified]:** EDGAR accepts filings Mon-Fri 06:00-22:00 ET. Filings that start after 17:30 ET are disseminated the next business day, except Forms 3/4/5, whose cutoff is 22:00 ET. Same source as above.
- **Licence:** US federal government works are generally public domain (17 U.S.C. §105) [unverified that SEC states this on a page I read]. Showing excerpts and derived data publicly is low-risk. Exhibits written by companies (press releases, decks) are company copyright, so quote short excerpts only.
- **XBRL quality traps [partly verified]:** the APIs return only standard taxonomies (us-gaap, ifrs-full, dei, srt) and facts for the whole entity, so custom tags are missing ([EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)). Known problems for small issuers [unverified, general experience]: inconsistent tags for cash vs. cash + short-term investments vs. marketable securities; YTD vs. quarterly duration mixing in 10-Qs; sign errors; FPIs reporting under IFRS. Advice: let the LLM read cash and runway from the 10-Q text and check it against XBRL, not the other way round.
- **Volume (whole universe, rough, my estimate) [unverified]:** ~60-100 filings per business day. Mostly Form 4 (tiny XML, <1k tokens), ~10-15 8-Ks/day (2-10 pages each plus exhibits), and bursts of 10-Q/10-K in earnings season (10-Q ~40-80 pages ≈ 30-60k tokens; 10-K ~120-200 pages ≈ 100-150k tokens). Send only the relevant sections (MD&A liquidity, going-concern note, risk-factor diff) to the LLM.

### 1.2 SEC form types: what each tells a small-biotech investor

| Form | What it tells you (plain language) | Timing / deadline | Price relevance (value) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10-K / 10-Q** | Cash, quarterly burn, "runway into [quarter/year]" language, **going-concern** doubt (management and/or auditor), risk-factor changes, pipeline status, debt covenants. 10-Q has no audit. | 10-K: 60/75/90 days after FY end depending on filer size; 10-Q: 40/45 days [unverified exact days; standard rule]. **Pending:** SEC proposed optional semiannual "Form 10-S" on 5 May 2026, not final as far as I found ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/05/sec-proposes-optional-semiannual-reporting-for), [Deloitte](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/publications/deloitte/heads-up/2026/sec-proposes-semi-annual-reporting)) [secondary] | **High**: going-concern and a shortening runway predict financing (dilution). First-time going-concern evidence in §4. Life-science prevalence: 36% (325) of public life-science companies got a GC opinion on 2023 statements per Audit Analytics as cited by [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/services/audit/biotech-financing-structures-and-accounting-implications.html) [secondary] |
| **8-K** | Unscheduled events. Items that matter: **1.01** material agreement (licensing/partnering deal, loan); **2.02** results (earnings release, usually exhibit 99.1); **3.01** delisting / non-compliance notice (e.g. Nasdaq $1 bid price); **5.02** CEO/CFO/CMO departures or appointments; **7.01** Reg FD (decks, conference slides); **8.01** other events (top-line trial data, FDA letters often go here); 1.03 bankruptcy; 2.05 restructuring/layoffs; 4.02 non-reliance (restatement). | Most items within **four business days**; 7.01/8.01 are voluntary and have no fixed deadline ([Investor.gov "How to read an 8-K"](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/how-read-8), [SEC readan8k.pdf](https://www.sec.gov/files/readan8k.pdf)) | **High**: best official, timestamped copy of material news |
| **S-3 / S-3ASR** | Shelf registration: the company can sell stock later. An ATM sales agreement is often filed with or right after it. S-3ASR = automatic shelf for well-known seasoned issuers (rare in small caps). **Baby-shelf rule (I.B.6):** float < $75m → may sell at most 1/3 of public float per 12 months, ATM sales included ([Hunton 2024](https://hunton.sitepilot11.firmseek.com/media/publication/200612_Publication-Sep-2024-Baby-Shelf-Rules-Explained.pdf), [Securities Law Blog May 2026 C&DI](https://securities-law-blog.com/2026/05/05/sec-publishes-cdi-on-atms-and-the-s-3-baby-shelf-rule/)) [secondary]. A June 2026 SEC proposal would drop the $75m float requirement ([Securities Law Blog](https://securities-law-blog.com/2026/06/10/sec-proposes-transformative-rule-changes-to-the-registered-offering-process-part-2/)) [secondary, proposal only] | When filed | **High**: an overhang signal; capacity left under the baby shelf caps dilution |
| **424B5 / 424B3 / 424B7** | Prospectus supplement: the actual offering (price, size, warrants) or a new or upsized ATM. Pairs with an 8-K/press release "pricing of public offering". | Usually within 2 business days of pricing [unverified, Rule 424(b)] | **High**: dilution as it happens; overnight offerings often price at a discount |
| **S-1 / F-1** | IPO or follow-on by companies not eligible for S-3; also resale registrations after PIPEs | When filed | Medium |
| **Form 4** | Insider buys/sells, option exercises, 10b5-1 plan flag (checkbox since 2023 [unverified]) | **Two business days** after the trade ([Harvard Law Forum comment letter](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2021/04/02/comment-letter-on-rule-144-holding-period-and-form-144-filings)) [secondary]; EDGAR cutoff 22:00 ET | **High** for open-market purchases (code P), especially clusters; sales are mostly noise |
| **Form 144** | Notice of a planned sale by an affiliate; electronic on EDGAR since 2023; filing hours extended to 22:00 ET ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/sec-extends-form-144-filing-hours)) [secondary] | Filed at or before the sale | Low-medium: early warning of insider selling |
| **13D / 13G** | >5% holders. 13D = active, 13G = passive. **2023 rules [secondary, consistent across firms]:** 13D initial filing within **5 business days** (was 10 days), amendments within **2 business days** (effective 5 Feb 2024). 13G (passive) initial within 5 business days; QII/exempt filers 45 days after quarter end; 13G amendments 45 days after quarter end; compliance 30 Sep 2024; structured-data (XML) requirement from 18 Dec 2024 ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2023/10/sec-adopts-changes-schedule-13d-schedule-13g/), [Vinson & Elkins](https://velaw.com/insights/shorter-schedule-13d-and-schedule-13g-filing-deadlines-and-new-guidance-sec-adopts-final-rules-amending-beneficial-ownership-reporting)). | **13F-HR** | Quarterly long US-listed positions of managers with ≥$100m in 13(f) securities. **Due 45 days after quarter end.** Long only, no shorts, no non-US listings, options may be reported, positions <10k shares and <$200k may be left out; confidential treatment possible (13F-CTR) ([SEC 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f)) [verified] | Quarterly, 45-day lag | **Medium**: slow, but specialist consensus is a documented (vendor) signal, see §4 |
| **NT 10-K / NT 10-Q** (Form 12b-25) | Late filing. The company gets +15 days (10-K) or +5 days (10-Q) and must give the reason; it often precedes restatements or GC trouble. Filed no later than 1 business day after the due date ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/keeping-current-disclosure-and-governance-developments/20230828-recent-sec-enforcement-activity-serves-as-a-reminder-that-rule-12b-25-requires-substantive-disclosures)) [secondary] | Event-driven | **Medium-high** as a red flag (rare) |

| **Form D** | Private placement notice (Reg D), filed within 15 days of first sale [unverified] | | Low-medium: PIPE detection, usually also in an 8-K |
| **SC TO-T / SC TO-I / SC 14D9, DEFM14A, 425** | Tender offers / M&A | Event-driven | High once a deal is announced, but then it is already priced in; useful for the case library |
| **CORRESP / UPLOAD (comment letters)** | SEC staff questions and company answers, released ≥20 business days after review completion [unverified] | Delayed | Low (occasionally revenue-recognition or trial-disclosure issues) |

### 1.4 Investor presentations and R&D days
- Corporate decks are PDFs on IR sites, often furnished as 8-K Item 7.01 exhibit 99.x on EDGAR (best: timestamped and immutable). IR sites overwrite their "latest deck" file, so **archive snapshots** yourself. Typical size 20-50 slides ≈ 5-15k tokens of text. **Legal position on downloading a public webcast and transcribing it locally (NOT legal advice)**
- *Copyright exists:* the recording and the spoken presentation are protected works of the company (and possibly the webcast provider).
- *EU / NL text-and-data-mining:* DSM Directive (EU) 2019/790 art. 2(2) defines TDM as "any automated analytical technique aimed at analysing text and data in digital form in order to generate information which includes but is not limited to patterns, trends and correlations". **Art. 3** (scientific research) covers only research organisations and cultural heritage institutions, so not a private hobbyist. **Art. 4** allows reproductions "of lawfully accessible works ... for the purposes of text and data mining", retained "for as long as is necessary", **unless "expressly reserved by their rightholders in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means"** for content online ([EUR-Lex 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj)) [verified]. NL implemented these as **art. 15n** (research) and **art. 15o** (general TDM with opt-out) of the Auteurswet ([Stb. 2020, 558](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stb-2020-558.html); [KVAN knowledge document, Apr 2026](https://www.kvan.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Kennisdocument-KVAN-Tekst-en-Datamining-in-de-Auteurswet.pdf)) [secondary; I could not load wetten.overheid.nl to quote the text]. Whether speech-to-text of an audio webcast followed by LLM analysis is "TDM" is plausible but untested [my reading, unverified]. Check the webcast page and robots.txt / TDM-reservation for an opt-out.
- *NL private copy (thuiskopie, art. 16c Auteurswet):* lets a natural person copy for "own practice, study or use" without commercial aim, but **not from an unlawful source** (CJEU *ACI Adam*, C-435/12, 2014) ([Kamerstuk 29838-72](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-29838-72.html)) [secondary]. A public, freely offered webcast is a lawful source. Whether 16c covers storing it for an automated system is untested [unverified].
- *Site terms (contract):* Q4's attendee FAQ says recordings exist only if the issuer bought them, and its subscription agreement restricts use of Q4 IP ([Q4 attendee FAQ](https://static.events.q4inc.com/docs/attendee/en/attendees/attendees--faq-.html), [Q4 MSA](https://www.q4inc.com/mastersubscriptionagreement)) [secondary]. I found **no explicit viewer-side ban on downloading**, but a site's terms can still forbid it, and circumventing DRM/technical protection is separately prohibited (art. 29a Auteurswet / DMCA §1201) [unverified].
- *US fair use (17 U.S.C. §107):* *Swatch Group v. Bloomberg*, 756 F.3d 73 (2d Cir. 2014) held that Bloomberg's recording and **distribution** of a full earnings call to subscribers was fair use: the factual, newsworthy content and the lack of a market for the work weighed in favour ([US Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/swatchgrp-bloomberg-2dcir2014.pdf)) [verified summary; one search snippet gave the citation as 742 F.3d 17, which is the earlier opinion]. Private transcription for analysis is a weaker infringement case than Bloomberg's.
- *Practical reading:* private download + local transcription + LLM analysis, with **no republication of the audio or full transcript**, is low-risk under both EU art. 4 / 15o (no reservation) and US fair use. Publishing short quotes with attribution in the public repo is normal quotation (art. 15a Auteurswet [unverified]). Publishing full transcripts is not. Check robots/TDM reservation and the page's terms before automating. **Owner decision.**
- *Conference presentations (JPM Healthcare, Jefferies, TD Cowen, Guggenheim...):* webcasts are hosted on IR sites, usually by the same providers (often Q4, Notified or Wsw.com) [unverified]. Slides are often furnished as 8-K 7.01 the same morning (JPM, mid-January) [unverified].

## 3. Fastest OPEN way to learn about material news (question 1)

Typical sequence for a small-biotech top-line release [mostly general knowledge, unverified unless cited]:
1. **Newswire** release, often 07:00-08:00 ET pre-market or 16:01 ET after close. Fastest, but PR Newswire bans robots and GlobeNewswire's terms are bot-hostile (§1.3). Business Wire allows "retrieving RSS feeds" [secondary].
2. **Company IR-site RSS:** IR platforms usually publish the same release at the same moment or within minutes [unverified]. Company-owned and offered for subscription. **Recommended primary trigger.**
3. **EDGAR 8-K** (Item 8.01/7.01 with exhibit 99.1): appears on sec.gov 1-3 minutes after acceptance ([Webmaster FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/about/webmaster-frequently-asked-questions)). Many issuers file within minutes to hours of the release, but the law allows up to four business days for most items, and Reg FD needs only "prompt" disclosure (one route is a broadly disseminated press release). Filings that start after 17:30 ET appear the next business day. **Recommended authoritative record** (immutable timestamp).

Practical design: poll ~200 IR RSS feeds every 2-5 min (≈1-2 requests/s total) + EDGAR getcurrent Atom (filtered to watched CIKs) every 1-2 min + per-CIK submissions JSON as backfill. Store the first-seen timestamp from every channel. This also measures the delays empirically during the pilot week.

## 5. Ranked top 8 by value per effort

1. **EDGAR submissions JSON + latest-filings Atom** (8-K, 424B5, S-3, Form 4, NT, 13D/G): high value, low effort, immutable timestamps.
2. **Form 4 parsing** (XML): open-market buy clusters; tiny documents, near-zero LLM cost.
3. **10-Q/10-K liquidity + going-concern + risk-factor diff** (EDGAR text, cross-check with XBRL companyfacts): high value, medium effort, largest token use (send sections only).
4. **Company IR-site RSS feeds**: fastest allowed news trigger; low-medium effort (collect 200 URLs once).
5. **Europe PMC REST** (papers + preprints, one keyless API): medium-high value, low effort.
6. **Conference embargo calendar + targeted abstract fetch** (ASCO, ASH, ESMO, AACR...): high value, high effort; do it as a calendar of drop times plus a manual/semi-automatic step at first.
7. **13D/13G + 13F of ~20 specialist funds**: medium value, low effort (quarterly).

## 6. Sources to avoid (and why)
- **PR Newswire automated polling**: terms ban robots/spiders, scraping and AI training ([terms](https://prnewswire.com/terms-of-use/)).
- **GlobeNewswire automated polling**, unless the owner accepts the ambiguity: §3.6 bans bots ([Notified terms](https://portal.notified.com/terms-conditions/en)).
- **Lens.org API**: institutional subscription only; investment decisions count as commercial ([terms](https://about.lens.org/lens-api-terms-of-use/)).
- **Paid transcript vendors** (Seeking Alpha, FMP, EarningsCall, AlphaSense...): closed, cost money, and their terms forbid redistribution.
- **Scraping Motley Fool transcripts, PubPeer, or conference abstract sites in bulk**: terms unclear or restrictive (ASH: no public republication).
- **PatentsView API**: paused since 20 Mar 2026; replacement not confirmed.
- **EDGAR full-text search (efts) as a primary feed**: undocumented as an API. Fine for occasional lookups, not for polling.
